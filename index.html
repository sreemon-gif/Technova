03 // TIMELINE

THE HISTORY OF COMPUTING

Computers didn't become powerful overnight. Explore the major technological milestones that transformed computing.

1940s

VACUUM TUBES

Early electronic computers used thousands of vacuum tubes. Machines were enormous and consumed huge amounts of power.

1950s

TRANSISTORS

Transistors began replacing vacuum tubes, allowing computers to become smaller, faster and more reliable.

1960s

INTEGRATED CIRCUITS

Multiple electronic components could now be placed on semiconductor chips.

1970s

MICROPROCESSORS

The CPU began appearing as a single integrated microprocessor, dramatically changing computing.

1980s

PERSONAL COMPUTERS

Computers became increasingly accessible to homes, schools and businesses.

1990s

THE INTERNET ERA

Networking and the World Wide Web transformed computers into communication and information platforms.

2000s

MULTI-CORE COMPUTING

CPUs increasingly used multiple processing cores, while GPUs became increasingly programmable.

2010s

SSD + MOBILE COMPUTING

Flash storage became mainstream while smartphones, laptops and cloud computing transformed everyday computing.

2020s

AI COMPUTING

AI accelerators, advanced GPUs, chiplets, high-bandwidth memory and specialized processors became increasingly important.

NEXT

THE FUTURE

Computing continues toward heterogeneous processors, advanced packaging, AI acceleration, optical interconnects and potentially new computing architectures.

04 // NEXT GENERATION

WHERE COMPUTERS ARE GOING

Some technologies are already being developed while others remain research directions. The future is not guaranteed — but these areas are worth watching.

🧠

AI ACCELERATORS

CPUs and GPUs are increasingly joined by specialized hardware designed to accelerate machine-learning workloads.

🧩

CHIPLET ARCHITECTURE

Instead of creating one enormous chip, designers can combine smaller chiplets into a larger processor system.

🧱

3D CHIP STACKING

Semiconductor components can be stacked vertically to increase density and shorten communication paths.

⚡

HIGH BANDWIDTH MEMORY

Stacked memory technologies can provide enormous memory bandwidth for demanding workloads such as AI and scientific computing.

💡

OPTICAL COMPUTING

Researchers are exploring photonic technologies that use light for parts of computation and communication.

⚛️

QUANTUM COMPUTING

Quantum computers use fundamentally different computational principles and may eventually solve certain specialized problems.
